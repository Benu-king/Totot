Our Story

From a shoeshine boy's dream to a national landmark

Totot was built by its owner and CEO, Tenkir Teni, who began as a shoeshine boy more than thirty years ago and grew it into one of the capital's top traditional restaurants.

Inside, dozens of waitresses in traditional attire serve Gurage-style dishes against a backdrop of live music and dance, in a hall celebrated for both its interior and exterior design.

The Signature · ክትፎ

Totot Kitfo

The dish Totot is known for. Finely minced beef, warmed with spiced clarified butter and mitmita, served the traditional way alongside ayib, a fresh cottage-style cheese plated on a banana leaf, with injera.

Guests also take it with gomen kitfo and qocho, the Gurage bread, for the full tradition.
